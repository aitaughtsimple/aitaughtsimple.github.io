Our curriculum

Six short lessons that take you from "What is this?" to using AI with confidence, and to spotting the scams that use it. Every lesson happens on your own phone, with a student helper beside you.

  • 6 lessons
  • iPhone or Android
  • About 1 hour per class
  • Free
  • Take-home cheat sheet

How we plan each class

Every class follows the same calm rhythm, and most of the time is hands-on. Hosts can shorten it to 45 minutes.

  1. Early10 min

    Help at the door

    Come a few minutes early for help getting on the wifi and making the text on your phone bigger.

  2. 0:005 min

    Welcome and today's plan

    We say hello, pair each person with a student helper, and share one rule: never type passwords, bank or card numbers, or your Social Security number into an AI chat. You can't break anything.

  3. 0:057 min

    Your first message

    Everyone sends their own first message to the AI, on their own phone, with a helper beside them.

  4. 0:128 min

    One example on the big screen

    We show one task from start to finish: asking, asking for a change, and catching a mistake the AI made.

  5. 0:2025 min

    Hands-on practice

    You pick a task from today's lesson and try it at your own pace with your helper. We finish with one "check it" step. Ask anything along the way.

  6. 0:457 min

    Safety minute

    Two "Real or scam?" examples, one real and one fake, so you can practice telling them apart.

  7. 0:528 min

    Show what you learned

    You show your helper one thing you can now do, fill in a short card with no name on it, and take home a cheat sheet.

The six lessons

Lesson 1 comes first. After that, a host can pick any lesson, and lesson 5 is part of every series.

  5. Lesson 5Spot AI scamsIn every series
  6. Lesson 6Keep info safe

  1. Lesson 1

    AI basics: what it is and how to ask

    Feel comfortable opening an AI chat app and having a simple conversation.

    You'll learn to

    • Understand what an AI chat tool does, in plain words
    • Open an AI assistant on your phone, like ChatGPT, Gemini, or Copilot, and start a new chat
    • Type or speak a question, then ask a follow-up
    • Make your phone easier to use: bigger text and voice typing

    Practice prompts

    Explain what you are in two simple sentences.

    Say that again, but simpler.

    Remember: AI can sound sure and still be wrong. Double-check anything important.

  2. Lesson 2

    Write emails and letters

    Get a clear, polite draft in seconds, then make it sound like you.

    You'll learn to

    • Say who the message is for and what you want to say
    • Ask for a version that's shorter, warmer, or more formal
    • Copy the text into your email or messages app

    Practice prompts

    Write a short, polite email to [my doctor's office] asking to move my [Tuesday] appointment.

    Make it warmer, and sign it from [Ruth].

    Change the words in [brackets] to fit you.

    Remember: Read every word before you send it. You're the author.

  3. Lesson 3

    Get quick answers

    Ask everyday questions in plain words and get clear, step-by-step answers.

    You'll learn to

    • Ask clear questions and request numbered steps
    • Ask "Can you explain that differently?" when it's confusing
    • Check important answers by seeing what other trusted sources say
    • Use AI to prepare questions for a doctor, lawyer, or banker, then ask them

    Practice prompts

    How do I make the text bigger on my phone? Give me numbered steps.

    How do I set a daily reminder on my phone?

    Remember: Never call a phone number an AI gives you. Get it from the company's official website, your bill, or your card.

  4. Lesson 4

    Plan trips and meals

    Turn a rough idea into a simple plan, a recipe, or a list.

    You'll learn to

    • Plan a day trip with stops and times
    • Turn what's in your fridge into an easy recipe
    • Make packing and shopping lists

    Practice prompts

    Plan a relaxed day trip to Annapolis for two people who don't want to walk much.

    I have chicken, rice, and spinach. Give me an easy dinner for two and a shopping list.

    Remember: Check hours, prices, and addresses on the official website, because AI can be out of date. Ask a pharmacist or doctor about food allergies or medicines.

  5. Lesson 5

    Spot AI scams

    Recognize fake calls, cloned voices, and phishing texts before they cost you anything.

    You'll learn to

    • Understand how voice cloning and the "grandparent scam" work, and why caller ID can be faked
    • Spot warning signs: rushing you, strange links, and asking for gift cards, crypto, or secrecy
    • Know that no real bank or agency tells you to move your money to a "safe" account
    • Set up a family safe word for emergency calls
    • Know what to do if you already paid or shared something

    Practice prompts

    I got this text: "Unpaid toll, pay today to avoid a fee." Does this look like a scam? Why?

    AI can point out warning signs, but it can't confirm a message is real. Check with the company using a number or website you already trust.

    Remember: If a call or message rushes you, asks for gift cards or crypto, or tells you to keep it secret, it's a scam. Hang up and call back on a number you know.

    Try the "Real or scam?" quiz

  6. Lesson 6

    Keep your personal info safe

    Use AI without giving away anything private.

    You'll learn to

    • Know what never to type into an AI chat
    • Use a temporary chat, and find the setting that stops your chats from being used to train the AI
    • Delete a chat, and know its limits: deleting helps, but the safest information is what you never typed
    • Ask questions without using real names or numbers

    Practice prompts

    What kinds of personal information should I never share with an AI chat tool?

    Remember: Treat an AI chat like a postcard. Never type passwords, bank or card numbers, or your Social Security number.
